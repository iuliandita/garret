# Timeline of Events

The narrative of the salt cartographer spans from autumn through the following spring, but it encompasses knowledge and predictions extending decades into the future. The central events occur within a single annual cycle, compressing great consequence into brief time.

The story begins in autumn, approximately three seasons after Maestro Kell's death during the autumn equinox. Ines works at the scriptorium, charting the Broken Rocks area where fishing boats frequently run aground. She discovers Maestro Kell's letter hidden beneath her easel, dated five days before his mysterious death. The letter directs her to find the copper-bound folder and study his hidden charts. This discovery initiates the entire sequence of conflicts and recognitions that follow. Within days of reading the letter, she locates the folder as Maestro Kell directed and begins the work of understanding his impossible charts.

In the weeks following her discovery, Ines brings the copper-bound folder to the tide-readers by the Narrows, who confirm that Maestro Kell's work aligns perfectly with their own fifteen years of patient observation. She also encounters the harbour master's official pressure to surrender the charts and meets Aurel Danse, the ambitious rival cartographer. By the end of autumn, Harbour Master Vetch issues his formal notice demanding all charting materials. Danse makes his explicit threat. Ines copies Maestro Kell's charts into her own hand to preserve the knowledge, understanding that she must be prepared to defend or hide the work.

The spring tide arrives in late winter or early spring, bringing visible physical changes to the coastline. A shale cliff on the outer islands develops a visible crack and begins to collapse. The water's reshaping work becomes unmistakable and immediate. Ines and Thren begin their systematic surveys of the western approaches, taking soundings and measuring the bay as it emerges. A substantial storm arrives, disrupting Danse's hurried survey and forcing his men to be rescued. During the storm, Ines experiences her profound realization about the salt marks and completes multiple fresh charts documenting not just what the bay is, but what the water intends it to become.

In the weeks following the storm, Ines publishes her complete survey under her own name. The tide-readers publish their confirming work two days later. Merchants immediately begin using her charts as the authoritative reference. The city council debates but eventually accepts her chart as official. Ines is offered and accepts the position of city cartographer. Aurel Danse, having lost his opportunity to acquire the charts quickly, withdraws from Meddow.

One year after the bay opened, approximately one full year from the spring tide's initial arrival, Ines stands at her workbench and begins charting the western approaches as she believes it will be ten years hence. She works in pencil, knowing her predictions will be wrong, understanding that this is the beginning, not the completion, of the endless conversation between cartographer and water that will continue across generations.
