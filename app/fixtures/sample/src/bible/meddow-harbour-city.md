# Meddow: Harbour City and Centre of Knowledge

Meddow is a small city built on the prosperity of coastal trade. The harbour is its heart, a place where merchant vessels arrive and depart on routes that carry goods between the three kingdoms. The city is organized around the water: harbour walls protect the inner anchorages, docks occupy the waterfront where cargo is loaded and unloaded, and the streets climb inland from the water's edge, winding through markets where fishmongers and merchants conduct their daily business. The harbour itself is protected by natural geography -- outer islands and reefs that make direct approach difficult but also make the inner anchorages secure once a ship has navigated the channels safely.

The city council maintains institutions necessary for trade to function. The harbour office keeps records of every passage and wreck and cargo manifesto accumulated over generations. Harbour Masters hold power through control of this information -- which routes are safe, which rocks are dangerous, which merchants can be trusted to repay debts or deliver goods on time. The official archives contain charts approved by the city council, representing accumulated knowledge deemed reliable enough to serve public navigation. These archives are kept by archivists like Mother Venn, who once worked the boats herself and brings practical understanding to the work of preservation.

Meddow's prosperity depends on accurate knowledge of the water. Merchants who have good charts know which routes are fastest, which are safest in different seasons, where deep anchorages exist. Merchants without this knowledge lose ships and cargo. This means that cartographic work, though not grand or publicly celebrated, holds genuine economic value. A skilled cartographer like Maestro Kell commands respect and can support a household through commissions from merchants who understand that the cost of the chart is small compared to the cost of losing a vessel.

The city also harbours private persons dedicated to understanding water patterns outside any official framework. The tide-readers live quietly by the Narrows, maintaining their own observations and charts without official sanction or payment. They represent a different approach to knowledge -- not commercial, not institutional, but devoted to understanding the water's deep patterns out of pure intellectual commitment. Both systems exist in Meddow: the official, sanctioned, merchant-serving knowledge preserved in the archives, and the deeper, patient knowledge pursued by those willing to wait decades for patterns to become clear. The story of the hidden bay reveals what happens when these two systems come into conflict.
